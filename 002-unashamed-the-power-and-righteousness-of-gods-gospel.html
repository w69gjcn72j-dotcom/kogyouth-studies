Unashamed: The Power and Righteousness of God’s Gospel

Romans 1:16–17 · June 2026
Big IdeaDon't be embarrassed by the gospel — it is God's power for salvation, and it shows God's righteousness from faith to faith.

Aim

  • (1) Students understand the gospel is God's power, not just good advice.
  • (2) Students grasp what God's righteousness means and why it is genuinely good news.
  • (3) Students take a step to trust God and pass on the faith to someone else.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB recommended), pens, 60-75 min

Connect (10 min)

Start with one or both of these before you open your Bibles.

  1. Have you ever been embarrassed about something you actually love? (A hobby, a show, a song, a food?) What made you hide it — and did you ever stop hiding it?
  2. If a mate was about to walk into real danger — like about to make a terrible decision — would you say something even if it was awkward? What might stop you?

Say: Paul is about to talk about something he is not embarrassed about at all. Let's find out why.

Read — Romans 1:16-17 (CSB)

For I am not ashamed of the gospel, because it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes, first to the Jew, and also to the Greek. For in it the righteousness of God is revealed from faith to faith, just as it is written: The righteous will live by faith.

Context

Paul is writing to Christians in Rome — the most powerful city in the world. In that culture, being linked to a crucified criminal would have looked pathetic and weak. Yet Paul says he is not ashamed, because the gospel is not just a nice message: it is God's actual power at work.

The word "righteousness" means being right with God. Paul's point is radical: God himself reveals what real rightness looks like in the gospel — and more than that, he provides it for us. It's not about being good enough. It's about trusting Jesus, who is.

Discuss

Work through these together. Take your time on the ones that get people talking.

  1. What does Paul say about the gospel in these two verses? List what it IS and what it DOES.
  2. Why might someone in Paul's day have been embarrassed by the gospel? Can you think of reasons people today might feel the same at school or with friends?
  3. Paul says the gospel is "the power of God for salvation" — not just helpful advice. What's the difference? Why does that change things?
  4. What does "salvation" mean? What are we being saved FROM, and what are we being saved FOR?
  5. What does it mean for something to be "righteous" or "right"? Think of a time something felt really unfair, or really just. What made it feel that way?
  6. Paul says God's righteousness is REVEALED in the gospel — shown and given by God, not earned by us. What does that tell us about how we get right with God?
  7. "The righteous will live by faith." What does it actually look like to live by trusting God — in your week at school, at home, in your group chat?
  8. The gospel spreads "from faith to faith" — one person's faith leads to another's. Who first brought the gospel to you or your family? Who might God want to use you to pass it on to?

Live It Out

The gospel spreads from faith to faith. Here's how to be part of that movement this week:

KOG 3·2·1·0

  • PRAY for 3 — Write down 3 people in your life who don't yet know Jesus. Pray for them by name every day this week. Ask God to use the gospel as his power in their lives.
  • CONNECT with 2 — Choose 2 of those 3 and make real effort to spend time with them. Don't lead with a sermon — just be a genuine friend.
  • INVITE 1 — Pick one person and invite them to something: youth group, church, a coffee, a conversation about life and faith.
  • 0 left out — Look around your school, team, or group chat. Is anyone on the edge? Make sure they know they matter.

Weekly challenge:

This week, notice when you feel embarrassed (or proud) about being a Christian. Pause and ask: "What do I actually believe this gospel is?" Then trust it anyway.

Memory verse:

"For I am not ashamed of the gospel, because it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes." Romans 1:16a (CSB)

PRAY (5-10 min)

Close your time together in prayer. Use these prompts:

  • Thank God for the gospel — not just words, but his actual power at work in people's lives, including yours.
  • Pray for the 3 people you wrote down — by name. Ask God to open their hearts and bring the gospel to them.
  • Ask God to make you bold and unashamed — that when the gospel comes up this week, you would trust it and not hide it.

Leader’s Notes

Framing this study

The goal is not to make students feel guilty for not being louder Christians. It's to show them something better: the gospel is GOD'S power, not ours. When we try to be bold in our own strength we fake it or burn out. When we see the gospel as what it actually is, being unashamed follows naturally. Point joyfully to Jesus throughout. Some students may not yet be Christians — never put them on the spot. Questions 7 and 8 are accessible to everyone in the group.

Key question notes

  • Q3: Advice can be ignored or disputed. Power does something. The gospel actually changes people from dead to alive — not self-improvement, new life. That's why Paul is bold.
  • Q6: God's righteousness isn't just a standard we fail — it's what he gives us through Jesus. He sends his perfectly righteous Son to die for the unrighteous, so that we can be made right before God. That's what makes it good news, not bad news.
  • Q8: "From faith to faith" — help students trace back who told them, who told that person. Martin Luther read this very passage and had a breakthrough: he realised God's righteousness is what God gives to believers, not what God punishes them with. Then flip it: who is God putting in their path this week?

Vision connection

KOG is a "from faith to faith" church. We're trusting God to grow as a congregation — not through slick programs but through the gospel doing what it does: giving life. Every student who prays for 3, connects with 2, and invites 1 is part of that movement. The 3·2·1·0 rhythm is one of the most direct ways to live out this passage.
