The Hypocrite and the Heart

Romans 2:17–3:8 · July 2026
Big IdeaDon't be hypocritical teachers whose falsehood breeds blasphemy; be people with Spirit-circumcised hearts, living out God's word for his praise and glory.

Aim

By the end of the study, students will (1) see that knowing about God is not the same as living for God, (2) understand that only the Holy Spirit can change a heart — and that Jesus kept God's law for us, and (3) commit to one small, concrete act of living out God's word this week.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, this sheet. About 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

Pick one (or both):

  • At bowling on Wednesday, some brothers chanted 'for shame, for shame' to put their older brother off his game. Be honest: have you ever called someone out for something you secretly do yourself? What happened?
  • Think of a product with amazing packaging that turned out to be a letdown inside — a game, a snack, a gadget. What was it? Why does the outside promise so much and the inside matter more?

Read — Romans 2:17–3:8 (CSB)

17 Now if you call yourself a Jew, and rely on the law, and boast in God, 18 and know his will, and approve the things that are superior, being instructed from the law, 19 and if you are convinced that you are a guide for the blind, a light to those in darkness, 20 an instructor of the ignorant, a teacher of the immature, having the embodiment of knowledge and truth in the law — 21 you then, who teach another, don't you teach yourself? You who preach, “You must not steal” — do you steal? 22 You who say, “You must not commit adultery” — do you commit adultery? You who detest idols, do you rob temples? 23 You who boast in the law, do you dishonour God by breaking the law? 24 For, as it is written: The name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of you.

25 Circumcision benefits you if you observe the law, but if you are a lawbreaker, your circumcision has become uncircumcision. 26 So if an uncircumcised man keeps the law's requirements, will not his uncircumcision be counted as circumcision? 27 A man who is physically uncircumcised, but who keeps the law, will judge you who are a lawbreaker in spite of having the letter of the law and circumcision. 28 For a person is not a Jew who is one outwardly, and true circumcision is not something visible in the flesh. 29 On the contrary, a person is a Jew who is one inwardly, and circumcision is of the heart — by the Spirit, not the letter. That person's praise is not from people but from God.

3:1 So what advantage does the Jew have? Or what is the benefit of circumcision? 2 Considerable in every way. First, they were entrusted with the very words of God. 3 What then? If some were unfaithful, will their unfaithfulness nullify God's faithfulness? 4 Absolutely not! Let God be true, even though everyone is a liar, as it is written: That you may be justified in your words and triumph when you judge. 5 But if our unrighteousness highlights God's righteousness, what are we to say? I am using a human argument: Is God unrighteous to inflict wrath? 6 Absolutely not! Otherwise, how will God judge the world? 7 But if by my lie God's truth abounds to his glory, why am I also still being judged as a sinner? 8 And why not say, just as some people slanderously claim we say, “Let us do what is evil so that good may come”? Their condemnation is deserved!

Context

Paul is writing to the church in Rome. In chapter 1 he showed God's anger against people who openly turn away from him. In chapter 2 he turns to the 'good' religious people — the Jews, who had God's law and the covenant sign of circumcision (which God gave Abraham in Genesis 17). Paul's point: having God's word and God's sign is a huge privilege, but privilege without practice dishonours God's name. What God wants is a changed heart — and only his Spirit can do that.

Discuss (30–35 min)

  1. Read verses 17–20 again. List the privileges the Jews had, and the things they were confident they could do for other people. Were they wrong about any of it?
  2. In verses 21–23, what examples does Paul give of people teaching one thing but doing another? Which one surprises you most?
  3. Verse 24: what happens to God's name when God's people don't live God's way? Why is that such a big deal?
  4. What does 'preaching but not practising' look like for a Christian at school or in the group chat? (Think: posting a Bible verse, then trash-talking someone in the same day.)
  5. Read verses 25–29. According to Paul, what makes someone a real member of God's people — the outside mark or something else? Who does the 'circumcising of the heart'?
  6. The sermon said we keep the law's requirements 'not because we're clever, not because we're good, but because Jesus has kept the law's requirements on our behalf.' How does that both humble us and comfort us?
  7. Read 3:3–4. If God's people fail him, does that cancel God's faithfulness? What does Paul's answer tell you about God?
  8. Read 3:8 and think about King David in Psalm 51. When your sin is exposed, what's the difference between making excuses and confessing? What stops us confessing — and what would help?

Live It Out

Don't just know the word — live the word. Start small, like the sister who simply gave a busy minister a cup of water because she'd read her Bible and lived it.

KOG 3·2·1·0 this week: PRAY for your 3 friends who don't know Jesus yet, CONNECT with 2 of them properly this week, INVITE 1 to youth group or church, and leave 0 people unloved — at school, at home, at church. Weekly challenge: Do one small, unasked act of kindness this week — a 'cup of water' moment. Say hi to someone at church you don't normally talk to and be genuinely interested in them. No one needs to notice. God does. Memory verse: “A person is a Jew who is one inwardly, and circumcision is of the heart — by the Spirit, not the letter. That person's praise is not from people but from God.” (Romans 2:29 CSB)

Pray (10 min)

  • Praise God that his faithfulness never fails — even when we are unfaithful, he stays true (Romans 3:3–4).
  • Confess like David, not like the objector. Take a quiet moment to own one sin before God — no excuses — and thank Jesus that he kept the law perfectly for you. Ask the Spirit to keep changing your heart.
  • Pray for your 3 friends by name — that God's kindness would lead them to repentance. Also pray for our church's next steps: three new mid-week fellowships, and six more qualified helpers for KOGKids, KOGMini and KOGYouth.

Leader's Notes

Warm framing: some in your group may not yet be Christians, and this passage can sound like 'church people are hypocrites — why bother?' Don't corner anyone. Paul's target is self-confident religion, not honest doubt. The good news is that God doesn't ask anyone to clean up their own heart — the Spirit circumcises hearts, and Jesus kept the law on our behalf. Point to him joyfully.

Q3 — God's name is blasphemed (v. 24, quoting Isaiah 52:5): when insiders don't live God's way, outsiders conclude God isn't worth following. Our lives are the world's first look at our God.

Q5 — The real mark is inward: a heart circumcised 'by the Spirit, not the letter' (v. 29). We can't operate on our own hearts; only God the Holy Spirit can take out the heart of stone and give a heart of flesh. A nice person isn't the same as a new heart.

Q6 — Keep this central: we're counted as law-keepers because Jesus, the perfect Son of God, kept God's law perfectly, and those who trust him are reckoned by God to have kept it. Gratitude, not fear, then drives obedience (Titus 2:11–12).

Q8 — David confessed with no excuses (Psalm 51), and God was justified in his judgement. Grace never gives permission to sin (3:8); it transforms us so we want to trust and obey. Model confession yourself — it will free your group to be honest.
