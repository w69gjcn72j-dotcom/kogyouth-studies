The Sinner in the Mirror

Romans 3:9–20 · July 2026
Big IdeaThe law of God exposes our true, utterly sinful selves — all alike under sin’s power, and not even one of us righteous — so that every mouth is stopped before the judgement of God.

Aim

By the end of this study, students will (1) see from Romans 3 that no one — not even a ‘good’ person — is righteous before God; (2) understand why the fear of God is a good thing that drives us to Jesus; (3) take one concrete step this week to ‘detox the tongue’ and pray for 3 friends who don’t know Jesus yet.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, this sheet, and about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

Pick one (or both) to get talking:

  • Filters. When you take a selfie, do you ever use a filter before you post it? Why do we like photos of ourselves that have been ‘fixed up’? What would it feel like if someone posted the unfiltered version?
  • Fear. Share about a time you were genuinely scared — lost in a shopping centre, a huge dog, an exam you forgot about. What did you do: fight, run away (flight), freeze, or try to sweet-talk your way out (fawn)?

Today’s passage says fear — one particular fear — is actually a good thing to have.

Read — Romans 3:9–20 (CSB)

9 What then? Are we any better off? Not at all! For we have already charged that both Jews and Greeks are all under sin, 10 as it is written: There is no one righteous, not even one. 11 There is no one who understands; there is no one who seeks God. 12 All have turned away; all alike have become worthless. There is no one who does what is good, not even one.

13 Their throat is an open grave; they deceive with their tongues. Vipers’ venom is under their lips. 14 Their mouth is full of cursing and bitterness. 15 Their feet are swift to shed blood; 16 ruin and wretchedness are in their paths, 17 and the path of peace they have not known. 18 There is no fear of God before their eyes.

19 Now we know that whatever the law says, it speaks to those who are subject to the law, so that every mouth may be shut and the whole world may become subject to God’s judgment. 20 For no one will be justified in his sight by the works of the law, because the knowledge of sin comes through the law.

Context

Paul has spent the first two chapters of Romans building a case: the Gentiles (non-Jews) are guilty before the holy God, and the Jews — who had God’s law — are guilty too, because they didn’t keep it. Now he sums up the whole case with a chain of quotes from the Old Testament (Psalms, Ecclesiastes, Isaiah). It reads like a doctor’s full-body check-up: throat, tongue, lips, mouth, feet, eyes — and the diagnosis is bad news for everyone, so that the good news of Jesus (which starts in v.21!) makes sense.

Discuss

  1. Look at v.9. Paul asks, ‘Are we any better off?’ What’s his answer? Who does he say is ‘under sin’ — and is anyone left out?
  2. Read vv.10–12 slowly. How many times does Paul say ‘no one’ or ‘not even one’? Why do you think he repeats it so much?
  3. In the middle of vv.11–12 is the heart of the argument: ‘There is no one who seeks God.’ According to these verses, what does a truly righteous person actually look like — and who measures up?
  4. Studies show 80–90% of drivers think they’re better than average — which is statistically impossible! Where do we do the same thing at school — ‘marking our own exam’ and rating ourselves as basically good people?
  5. In vv.13–18 Paul does a head-to-toe examination: throat, tongue, lips, mouth, feet, eyes. What does each body part do wrong? Why do you think sin shows up in what we say more than almost anywhere else?
  6. The final line of the diagnosis is v.18: ‘There is no fear of God before their eyes.’ We usually think fear is bad — but Proverbs 1:7 says the fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge. Why would fearing God actually be a good thing?
  7. Look at vv.19–20. What does God’s law do — and what can it never do? Why will every mouth be ‘shut’ on judgement day, like a smuggler at customs shown the evidence on Border Security?
  8. Jesus is the only person who feared God perfectly. In the garden before the cross he was so distressed he sweated drops like blood — yet he prayed, ‘Not my will but yours be done.’ How does Jesus facing God’s judgement in our place change how we look in the mirror of God’s word?

Live It Out

God’s word is a mirror with no filters. It shows you as God sees you — and then it shows you Jesus, who took the judgement you deserve. Here’s how to live that this week:

  • Detox the tongue. Romans 3 says sin lives in throats, tongues and lips — and for us that includes group chats. Before you send or post, pause: is this bitter, mocking or toxic? Choose silence over toxic speech. Think before you type.
  • Look in the mirror daily. James says don’t be someone who looks in the mirror and walks away doing nothing. Read a few verses of the Bible each day this week and pray honestly about what it shows you.
  • KOG 3·2·1·0. Pray for your 3 friends who don’t know Jesus yet, connect with 2 people at church or youth group this week, invite 1 friend to KOGYouth or church — and leave 0 people unloved or left out at school.
This week’s challenge: Every time you’re about to fire back a harsh message or join in trashing someone in a chat — stop, put your hand over your mouth (or your thumbs off the keyboard!), and pray one sentence for that person instead. Memory verse: “For no one will be justified in his sight by the works of the law, because the knowledge of sin comes through the law.” — Romans 3:20 (CSB)

Pray

  • Confess together. Thank God that his word is an unfiltered mirror. Confess that none of us is righteous — not even one — and thank Jesus for facing God’s judgement in our place.
  • Pray for your 3 by name. Go around the group and each pray for your three friends who aren’t Christians yet — that God’s kindness would lead them to repentance, and for a chance to point one of them to Jesus.
  • Pray for our church. Ask God to raise up six additional qualified helpers for KOGKids, KOGMini and KOGYouth this quarter, and to grow our three new mid-week groups — and that our church family would be people who walk the path of peace together.

Leader’s Notes

Warm framing: some of your students may not yet be Christians — and this passage says that apart from God’s grace, none of us would ever seek him! So if anyone in your group is even curious about God, that’s a miracle of God’s kindness. Never corner anyone; joyfully point to Jesus. The tone is not ‘you’re terrible’ but ‘we’re all in the same boat — and Jesus got in the boat to rescue us.’

Question helps

  • Q1: ‘We’ most naturally refers to Christians (following vv.7–8) — so even Christians are not better people than anyone else; we’re forgiven, not superior. Jews, Greeks, everyone: all under sin.
  • Q3: The verses form a sandwich (a chiasm): ‘no one righteous / no one does good’ on the outside, and at the centre, ‘no one seeks God.’ Righteousness is defined by relationship: a righteous person seeks God and does what God wants. Nobody does — which is why anyone seeking God today is God’s own doing.
  • Q5: Draw out that speech reveals the heart — like a doctor saying ‘open your mouth and say ahh.’ An ‘open grave’ is deep, unclean and deadly. For teens the sharpest application is online: gossip, pile-ons, ‘jokes’ that belittle.
  • Q6: The fear of God is seeing ourselves as we really are — sinners facing God’s judgement — and letting that drive us to our knees and to Jesus. Fighting God, fleeing him, freezing, or faking praise (fight/flight/freeze/fawn) all fail; falling on our knees is the one right response.
  • Q7: The law is like a straight-edge that shows how crooked we are, or the evidence photo at customs. It diagnoses sin; it cannot cure it. No one, in any era, is saved by keeping the law — salvation has always been by grace.
  • Q8: Keep the gospel note ringing: v.20 is the dark backdrop for v.21 (‘But now… the righteousness of God has been revealed apart from the law’) — which is next week! Jesus alone feared God perfectly and faced God’s wrath for us.
