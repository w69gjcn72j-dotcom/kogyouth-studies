Hope Against Hope

Romans 4:1–25 · August 2026
Big IdeaBe fully and joyfully convinced, hoping against hope, in the God who credits righteousness to those who trust in him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead.

Aim

By the end of this study, students will (1) see that God credits righteousness to people who trust him, never to people who earn it; (2) understand that real faith is being fully convinced that God can do what he has promised, even when everything looks dead; (3) take one concrete step this week to trust God out loud and pray for 3 friends who don't know Jesus yet.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, this sheet, and about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

Pick one (or both) to get talking:

  • Who gets the credit? Think of a group assignment where someone else took the credit for your work — or where you got credit you didn't really earn. How did it feel? Why do we care so much about credit?
  • Against the odds. Tell about a time you kept hoping for something when everyone said it was over — a game, a comeback, a result. Were you right to keep hoping, or were you just being stubborn?

Today's passage is about a man who was credited — by God himself. And about the kind of hope that keeps going when there is nothing left to look at.

Read — Romans 4:1–25 (CSB)

1 What then will we say that Abraham, our forefather according to the flesh, has found? 2 If Abraham was justified by works, he has something to boast about — but not before God. 3 For what does the Scripture say? Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him for righteousness. 4 Now to the one who works, pay is not credited as a gift, but as something owed. 5 But to the one who does not work, but believes on him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is credited for righteousness.

6 Just as David also speaks of the blessing of the person to whom God credits righteousness apart from works: 7 Blessed are those whose lawless acts are forgiven and whose sins are covered. 8 Blessed is the person the Lord will never charge with sin.

9 Is this blessing only for the circumcised, then? Or is it also for the uncircumcised? For we say, Faith was credited to Abraham for righteousness. 10 In what way, then, was it credited — while he was circumcised, or uncircumcised? It was not while he was circumcised, but uncircumcised. 11 And he received the sign of circumcision as a seal of the righteousness that he had by faith while still uncircumcised. This was to make him the father of all who believe but are not circumcised, so that righteousness may be credited to them also. 12 And he became the father of the circumcised, who are not only circumcised but who also follow in the footsteps of the faith our father Abraham had while he was still uncircumcised.

13 For the promise to Abraham or to his descendants that he would inherit the world was not through the law, but through the righteousness that comes by faith. 14 If those who are of the law are heirs, faith is made empty and the promise nullified, 15 because the law produces wrath. And where there is no law, there is no transgression. 16 This is why the promise is by faith, so that it may be according to grace, to guarantee it to all the descendants — not only to the one who is of the law but also to the one who is of Abraham's faith. He is the father of us all. 17 As it is written: I have made you the father of many nations — in the presence of the God in whom he believed, the one who gives life to the dead and calls things into existence that do not exist.

18 He believed, hoping against hope, so that he became the father of many nations according to what had been spoken: So will your descendants be. 19 He did not weaken in faith when he considered his own body to be already dead (since he was about a hundred years old) and also the deadness of Sarah's womb. 20 He did not waver in unbelief at God's promise but was strengthened in his faith and gave glory to God, 21 because he was fully convinced that what God had promised, he was also able to do. 22 Therefore, it was credited to him for righteousness.

23 Now it was credited to him was not written for Abraham alone, 24 but also for us. It will be credited to us who believe in him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead. 25 He was delivered up for our trespasses and raised for our justification.

Context

Paul has just finished proving that everyone — religious or not — is guilty before God, and that God makes people right with himself as a free gift through Jesus (3:21–31). Now someone objects: what about Abraham? He was the Jews' greatest hero. One ancient Jewish writer even claimed “Abraham was perfect in all his deeds with the Lord”. If anyone earned a good standing with God, surely he did.

Paul goes back to Genesis and shows the opposite. Abraham worshipped idols for most of his life before he was 75, and lied more than once to save himself — even putting his wife in danger. He had nothing to boast about. What he had was trust. And God counted that trust as righteousness.

Discuss

  1. Verse 1 says Abraham “found” something. Read vv.1–3. What did Abraham discover about himself — and why is that surprising, given he was the great hero of the faith?
  2. Verses 4–5 compare a wage with a gift. What is the difference? Why does it matter so much that God's righteousness comes as a gift and not as pay?
  3. Verse 5 says God “justifies the ungodly”. Does that sound right to you? Look at Romans 3:25–26 — how can God do that and still be a good judge?
  4. In vv.6–8 David says the forgiven person is “blessed” — that is, happy. What is the difference between being happy because everything is going well, and being happy because God will never charge you with your sin?
  5. Verses 9–12 make a point about timing: Abraham was counted righteous years before he was circumcised. What does that tell us about who gets included in God's family — and who gets to belong at church?
  6. Verse 15 says “the law produces wrath”. If God's law is good, why is it bad news for us? What does it show us that we would never see otherwise?
  7. Read vv.18–21. Abraham was about a hundred, and Sarah's womb was “dead”. Everything he knew said no. What kept him from wavering — and what does that tell you faith actually is?
  8. Verse 25: Jesus was “delivered up for our trespasses and raised for our justification”. If that is true, what changes about how you handle being embarrassed, left out, or judged at school this week?

Live It Out

Faith is not a smaller, easier thing that God lowered the bar to. Trusting God completely is harder than relying on yourself, because it means giving him all the credit and keeping none. Here is what that looks like this week:

  • Change the question. Every time you catch yourself asking “What have I achieved for God?”, swap it for “What has God credited to me in Christ?” Righteousness. Forgiveness. A Father. Nothing you earned.
  • Give glory before you see it. Pick one thing you have been praying about that looks dead. Thank God for it now — before anything changes — the way Abraham gave glory to God with nothing to show for it (v.20).
  • KOG 3·2·1·0. Pray for your 3 friends who don't know Jesus yet, connect with 2 people at church or youth group this week, invite 1 friend to KOGYouth, church or Father's Day (6 September) — and leave 0 people unloved or left out at school.
This week's challenge: When something goes wrong and you are tempted to say “there's no point praying about this” — pray about it anyway, out loud, and tell one person you did. That is hoping against hope.

Memory verse: “He did not waver in unbelief at God's promise but was strengthened in his faith and gave glory to God, because he was fully convinced that what God had promised, he was also able to do.” — Romans 4:20–21 (CSB)

  • Pray for your 3 by name. Go around the group and each pray for your three friends who aren't Christians yet — that God's kindness would lead them to repentance, and for a chance to point one of them to Jesus.

Leader's Notes

Warm framing: some of your students may not yet be Christians, and this chapter is a gift to them — the God of Romans 4 justifies the ungodly, not the sorted. The tone is not “believe harder” but “look at who God is: the one who gives life to the dead”. Never corner anyone. Let the passage do the work.

Expect one of two wrong turns. Some students will hear “faith not works” and conclude that obedience doesn't matter; others will quietly turn faith itself into a work they must produce. Verse 21 answers both: faith is being convinced about God, so it looks outward at him, not inward at itself.

Question helps

  • Q1: Abraham “found” that he had nothing to boast about — not before God, and honestly not before people either. The hero of the faith came with empty hands like everyone else.
  • Q2: Wages are owed; gifts are given. Nobody writes a thank-you card for their salary, and nobody invoices you for a birthday present. If righteousness were pay, God would owe us — and grace would not be grace.
  • Q3: God does not justify the guilty by ignoring the guilt. At the cross he presented Jesus as the atoning sacrifice (3:25), so he is both just and the one who justifies. Free for us; not cheap for God.
  • Q4: Blessed means happy — happy because God is happy with you. The USB-drive picture helps: imagine the evidence of your worst moment handed over and destroyed because someone else was sentenced in your place. Your circumstances can be terrible and this still holds.
  • Q5: Abraham was counted righteous while still uncircumcised; circumcision came later as a seal, not a qualification. So he is father of all who believe. Practically: our welcome cannot be limited to people who have sorted their lives out.
  • Q6: The law is good — it shows us the line and tells us when we have crossed it. But that is bad news for line-crossers: it produces wrath. Its good work is to drive us to the promise.
  • Q7: Abraham was “fully convinced that what God had promised, he was also able to do” (v.21). Faith is not optimism or refusing to look at the facts — he did consider his body (v.19). It is being more sure of God than of the odds.
  • Q8: If your standing before God is settled by the risen Jesus, the verdict at school is not the verdict that counts. Watch for students who are quietly living for the approval of people; this is where the gospel lands.
