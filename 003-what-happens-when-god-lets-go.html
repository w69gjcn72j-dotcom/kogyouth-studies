What Happens When God Lets Go

Romans 1:18–32 · July 2026
Big IdeaEveryone already knows God is real — and keeps pushing that truth down. When people keep doing that, God's judgment can look like letting them have exactly what they wanted: life fully without him. Jesus is the only way off that road.

Aim

  • — by the end, students will be able to:
  • Explain what "God gave them over" means in Romans 1.
  • Recognise "functional atheism" — living like God doesn't exist — in their own lives.
  • See Jesus as the only rescue from that downward spiral, not their own effort.

You’ll need: Bibles, pens, and 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

The Ignore Button

Everyone has something they know is true but tries hard not to think about — an unanswered text, a grade you're avoiding checking, a habit you know isn't good for you. Go around the group: what's one small thing (nothing too deep!) you've been ignoring lately?

Running on Autopilot

Think of a time you knew a rule — a curfew, a deadline, a coach's instruction — and broke it anyway, then had to deal with what happened next (not the telling-off, just the natural result). What happened?

Read

Read Romans 1:18-32 aloud (CSB) — have two or three students each take a few verses.

Context

Paul is writing to Christians in Rome, laying the foundation for the whole letter. Before he explains the good news of the gospel in detail, he shows why everyone — not just "obviously bad people" — desperately needs it. Romans 1:18-32 describes what happens when people keep pushing away what they already know about God: eventually, God "gives them over" to the full weight of their own choices.

Discuss

  1. Look at verses 19-20. How does Paul say God has made himself known to everyone, everywhere — even people who've never opened a Bible?
  2. Circle it: verses 24, 26 and 28 all use the same phrase — "God gave them over." What do you think that phrase means?
  3. The sermon this week said there's no such thing as a true atheist — everyone deep down knows God is real. Do you agree? Why do people still act like he isn't?
  4. Verses 21-23 describe what happens right before God "gives someone over": they stop thanking God, and their thinking gets foolish. What does that look like in real life?
  5. The sermon compared God "giving people over" to what a loving parent sometimes has to do with a strong-willed teenager — stepping back and letting them feel the weight of their own choices. Why would a loving God do something that looks like this?
  6. "Everybody's a functional atheist sometimes" — living like God doesn't exist, even if we'd never say we don't believe in him. Where do you catch yourself doing that? (Your phone? What you watch? A friendship you know isn't good for you?)
  7. Verses 29-31 list sins that are true of humanity in general — gossip, arrogance, disobeying parents, and more. Which one is honestly hardest for you to admit is true of you?
  8. This passage is heavy news. But Paul already told us in verse 17: "the righteous will live by faith." How does knowing Jesus change the way we read a passage like this — as a diagnosis we need, not a condemnation we're stuck under?

Live It Out

This term, St Paul's is living out KOG 3·2·1·0 — pray for 3, connect with 2, invite 1, 0 left unloved. If Romans 1 is right that everyone already knows the truth about God deep down but keeps pushing it away, then the most loving thing you can do for your 3 people is to keep gently pointing them back to Jesus — not write them off.

  • Pray for 3: write down 3 names of people who don't yet know Jesus.
  • Connect with 2: pick 2 of them to check in with properly this week — not just a like on their story.
  • Invite 1: invite 1 of them to something — church, youth group, or just to talk about faith.
  • 0 left unloved: notice if anyone in your own group or friend circle feels left out, and go include them.

Weekly Challenge: Catch yourself in one "functional atheist" moment this week — a moment you're tempted to just push God out of the picture — and instead, pray about it on the spot. Even 10 seconds counts.

Memory Verse: Romans 1:16 (CSB) — "For I am not ashamed of the gospel, because it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes."

Pray

  • Pray by name for your 3 — that God would keep working on their hearts and draw them from pushing him away to trusting him.
  • Pray for honesty — ask God to show each of you one place you're living like a functional atheist, and for the courage to change it.
  • Pray for KOGYouth — for the new assistant leaders being recruited and trained this term, and for anyone in the group who's unsure about faith to feel welcomed, never judged.

Leader's Notes

This is a heavy passage, and some students in your group may not yet be Christians, or may have family or friends who fall into one of the categories Paul lists. Handle it with grace. Paul's whole point across Romans 1–3 is that everyone is somewhere on this list — it's not a passage for singling anyone out. Your job tonight is simply to joyfully point students to Jesus. Never let this become a moment where someone feels cornered or targeted.

Q3 — if it stalls, point to C.S. Lewis's observation in Mere Christianity: people everywhere have "a curious idea that they ought to behave in a certain way, and cannot really get rid of it" — and yet don't behave that way. That gap between what we know and how we live is universal, not just "other people's" problem.

Q5 — the aim isn't to make God sound harsh. He's not being vindictive; he's letting people experience the truth about a life without him, because sometimes that's the only way people turn back. It's a severe mercy, not abandonment for its own sake.
